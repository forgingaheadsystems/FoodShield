# FoodShield Works Cited

This register preserves sources used for FoodShield terminology, requirements analysis, procedures, and design decisions. Each entry records the issuing authority, the reason it is relevant, and the date it was verified. A citation documents evidence; it does not by itself make every provision applicable to FoodShield or to every organization using it.

**Register last updated:** 2026-11-12

The update date is not a blanket claim that every linked page was reopened on
that date. Sources used in the current external-terminology map were rechecked
on 2026-11-12. Other retained sources must be rechecked when a later decision
depends on their current state.

## Project scope and terminology method

- Sundermeir, Samantha, et al. “Current Use and Demand for Digital Tools to Enhance Food Pantry Management: Findings from a Nationwide Survey.” *Journal of Hunger & Environmental Nutrition*, vol. 19, no. 6, 2024, pp. 938–951. DOI: [10.1080/19320248.2023.2228728](https://doi.org/10.1080/19320248.2023.2228728). [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39931051/).
  **Use:** Primary source for the survey features, reported demand, and distinctions such as client choice versus online ordering.
  **Limit:** The article reports needs and interest; it is not a complete software requirements specification.

- International Organization for Standardization. [ISO 704:2022, *Terminology work — Principles and methods*](https://www.iso.org/standard/79077.html).
  **Use:** Supports concept-, definition-, and designation-based terminology work.
  **Limit:** It does not prescribe FoodShield’s file layout or the local disposition labels.

- National Institute of Standards and Technology. [NIST IR 8477, *Mapping Relationships Between Documentary Standards, Regulations, Frameworks, and Guidelines*](https://www.nist.gov/publications/mapping-relationships-between-documentary-standards-regulations-frameworks-and).
  **Use:** Confirms that “concept crosswalk” and concept mapping are established terminology for documenting relationships among concepts.
  **Limit:** `FoodShield-to-Industry Terminology Map` is a Codex-proposed project label approved by the owner; NIST does not require that title or this repository's format.

- U.S. Census Bureau. [Industry and Occupation Code Lists & Crosswalks](https://www.census.gov/topics/employment/industry-occupation/guidance/code-lists.html).
  **Use:** Provides another authoritative example of crosswalks connecting classification systems.
  **Limit:** Census classification crosswalks do not establish FoodShield terminology or requirements.

- Feeding America. [“What is the difference between a food bank and food pantry?”](https://www.feedingamerica.org/find-your-local-foodbank).
  **Use:** Current sector explanation distinguishing food banks from food pantries.
  **Limit:** Program-specific legal definitions may differ.

## Government food programs and funding

- Office of the Federal Register. [7 CFR 251.3 — Definitions](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-B/part-251/section-251.3).
  **Issuer:** USDA Food and Nutrition Service.
  **Use:** TEFAP terms including distribution site, eligible recipient agency, emergency feeding organization, food bank, food pantry, and State agency.
  **Limit:** These meanings control in the scope of 7 CFR part 251; they must not be applied automatically outside TEFAP.

- Office of the Federal Register. [2 CFR 200.1 — Definitions](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-A/section-200.1).
  **Issuer:** Office of Management and Budget.
  **Use:** Federal-award terms including recipient, subrecipient, pass-through entity, contractor, participant, Federal award, and notice of funding opportunity.
  **Limit:** Program-specific statutes or regulations can take precedence.

- Office of the Federal Register. [2 CFR 200.331 — Subrecipient and contractor determinations](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-D/section-200.331).
  **Issuer:** Office of Management and Budget.
  **Use:** Distinguishes subaward relationships from procurement relationships.
  **Limit:** Classification is case-specific and based on substance, not merely the agreement’s label.

- U.S. General Services Administration. [SAM.gov Entity Registration](https://sam.gov/entity-registration).
  **Use:** Distinguishes a Unique Entity ID from active SAM.gov registration and explains the registration needed to apply directly for Federal awards.
  **Limit:** Registration requirements depend on the transaction and award role.

- Internal Revenue Service. [Exemption requirements for 501(c)(3) organizations](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/exemption-requirements-501c3-organizations).
  **Use:** Distinguishes Federal tax-exempt charitable status from ordinary use of “nonprofit” or “charitable.”
  **Limit:** This is not a determination of FoodShield’s entity status.

## Transportation, trucking, and warehousing

- Office of the Federal Register. [21 CFR 1.904 — Definitions for sanitary transportation](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-O/section-1.904).
  **Issuer:** U.S. Food and Drug Administration.
  **Use:** Controlling regulatory definitions for sanitary-transportation functions including carrier, loader, receiver, and shipper.
  **Limit:** These functional definitions apply within 21 CFR part 1, subpart O and do not replace FMCSA business classifications or traceability-event definitions.

- U.S. Food and Drug Administration. [FSMA Final Rule on Sanitary Transportation of Human and Animal Food](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-sanitary-transportation-human-and-animal-food).
  **Use:** Identifies covered functions including shipper, loader, carrier, and receiver and the sanitary-transportation framework.
  **Limit:** Coverage and exemptions depend on the operation and food involved.

- U.S. Food and Drug Administration. [Frequently Asked Questions on FSMA](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/frequently-asked-questions-fsma).
  **Use:** Current FDA explanations of shipper, loader, carrier, receiver, reassigned responsibilities, and multi-role entities.
  **Limit:** Guidance and FAQs do not replace the controlling regulation.

- Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. [Definitions of motor carrier, broker, and freight forwarder authorities](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/faq/what-are-definitions-motor-carrier-broker-and-freight-forwarder-authorities).
  **Use:** Distinguishes physical transportation, arranging transportation, and assuming responsibility for transportation.
  **Limit:** Registration and authority requirements depend on the actual operation.

- Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. [Broker and freight-forwarder registration FAQ](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/faq/must-freight-forwarders-and-brokers-register-fmcsa).
  **Use:** Records that operating in more than one role can require more than one registration.
  **Limit:** It is not a classification of any FoodShield participant.

## Produce, donation, and traceability

- Office of the Federal Register. [21 CFR 1.1310 — Food Traceability Rule definitions](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-S/section-1.1310).
  **Issuer:** U.S. Food and Drug Administration.
  **Use:** Definitions including critical tracking event, key data element, Food Traceability List, holding, receiving, shipping, transformation, transporter, traceability lot, and traceability lot code.
  **Limit:** These are definitions for 21 CFR part 1, subpart S. The rule’s definition of shipping expressly excludes donation of surplus food.

- U.S. Food and Drug Administration. [FSMA Final Rule on Requirements for Additional Traceability Records for Certain Foods](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods).
  **Use:** Current scope, implementation resources, and enforcement-date information for the Food Traceability Rule.
  **Current timing note:** The original compliance date was January 20, 2026; FDA states that Congress directed it not to enforce the rule before July 20, 2028.
  **Limit:** Non-enforcement timing does not erase the regulation or establish whether a specific organization is covered.

- U.S. Food and Drug Administration. [Traceability Lot Code](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/traceability-lot-code).
  **Use:** Explains the relationship among traceability lots, codes, code sources, and associated key data elements.
  **Limit:** FDA does not prescribe one technology or one code format.

- Office of the Federal Register. [21 CFR 112.3 — Produce Safety Rule definitions](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-112/subpart-A/section-112.3).
  **Issuer:** U.S. Food and Drug Administration.
  **Use:** Definitions including produce, covered activities, harvesting, packing, holding, and manufacturing/processing.
  **Limit:** “Produce” and “covered produce” are not interchangeable, and applicability depends on the activity and entity.

- USDA Agricultural Marketing Service. [PACA Licensing](https://www.ams.usda.gov/rules-regulations/paca/licensing).
  **Use:** Current USDA description of licensing thresholds and produce-business roles under the Perishable Agricultural Commodities Act.
  **Limit:** Licensing is fact-specific; FoodShield must not infer that a pantry, donor, trucker, or other user is a dealer, broker, commission merchant, or growers’ agent.

- USDA Agricultural Marketing Service. [PACA Common Terms](https://www.ams.usda.gov/rules-regulations/paca/common-terms).
  **Use:** Produce-trade meanings including acceptance, commercial unit, consignment, delivered, F.O.B., good delivery, grade contract, quality defect, and condition defect.
  **Limit:** These terms apply to produce transactions and contracts; they are not general inventory labels.

- U.S. Department of Agriculture. [Frequently Asked Questions about the Bill Emerson Good Samaritan Food Donation Act](https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/documents/usda-good-samaritan-faqs.pdf).
  **Use:** Donation terms including apparently wholesome food, apparently fit grocery product, donor, gleaner, nonprofit organization, and ultimate distribution to needy individuals.
  **Limit:** The source expressly states that it is not legal advice and does not override State or local health requirements.

## Supply-chain interoperability

- GS1. [GS1 Global Traceability Standard](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/global-traceability/).
  **Use:** Industry framework for interoperable traceability events and data.
  **Limit:** GS1 is a trade standard, not a universal government mandate.

- GS1 US. [Standards at a glance](https://www.gs1us.org/industries-and-insights).
  **Use:** Definitions and roles of GTIN, GLN, SSCC, and other supply-chain identifiers.
  **Limit:** Adoption and licensing requirements must be evaluated against actual trading-partner needs.

- U.S. Food and Drug Administration. [Food Traceability Rule FAQs](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/frequently-asked-questions-fsma-food-traceability-rule).
  **Use:** FDA states that it does not require or endorse a particular technology or standard, while recognizing that standards such as GS1 can support interoperable data exchange.
  **Limit:** Using GS1 does not by itself establish compliance with the Food Traceability Rule.
